The shape and elastic properties of various body cells are known to change during disease. For
example, changes in cell deformability have been reported for various cancerous body tissues
[1]. Red blood cells (RBCs) exhibit remarkable rheological characteristics that are particularly important for th eir biological function of oxygen delivery to tissue and removal of carbon dioxide through the vascular system in a wide range of blood flow conditions [2]. Alterations of mechanical properties of human RBCs have been reported for peripheral vascular disease [3],sickle cell anemia [4], malaria [5,6], diabetes mellitus [7], sepsis and renal failure [8].
Optical methods for the diagnosis of blood diseases are a current topic of research. For example, it was recently shown that different anemias can be identified holographically by modeling RBCs as microlenses [9].
